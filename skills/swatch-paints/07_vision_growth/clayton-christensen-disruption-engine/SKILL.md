## 3. PURPOSE

This skill equips Swatch Paints Strategic Planners, R&D Chemists, and Product Innovation Teams with Clayton Christensen’s **Disruptive Innovation and Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Methodologies**.

In the Indian paint industry, incumbent multinational companies fall into the classic "Innovator's Dilemma":
- They constantly add complex features, expensive packaging, and celebrity advertising to push upmarket into ultra-luxury interior paints (chasing higher margins).
- In doing so, they over-serve and abandon the massive base of rural and semi-urban homeowners who need simple, tough, weather-resistant exterior paint.
- Local blenders fill the vacuum with terrible, unstandardized paints that peel in 6 months, leaving millions of budget-conscious homeowners frustrated.

The purpose of this engine is to:
- Identify pools of **Non-Consumption and Over-Served Customers** in Tier 3/4 markets.
- Uncover the emotional, functional, and social **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)**.
- Engineer **Asymmetric Formulations** that deliver high durability at accessible price points.
- Build an unassailable low-cost operating model that incumbents cannot copy without destroying their own gross margins.

## 6. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

1. What job is the customer really "hiring" this paint product to do?
2. Are incumbent multinational competitors over-serving this market with complex, overpriced products?
3. Can we formulate a product that is simpler, 25% more affordable, yet delivers 100% of the required functional job?
4. If an incumbent saw our low-end product, would their reaction be to fight us, or to gladly abandon the market to pursue higher-margin luxury products?
5. What are the non-consumption barriers in this region (e.g. high price, lack of tinting machines, complicated application)?
6. Does our manufacturing cost structure allow us to make 30%+ gross margins at this entry-level price point?
7. How does the social prestige job (impressing neighbors) interact with the functional job (stopping water leaks)?
8. Are we designing for the way rural painters actually work (e.g. diluting with uncalibrated tap water, using rough brushes)?
9. How can we combine two separate steps into one (e.g. 2-in-1 Primer + Topcoat) to save contractor labor?
10. Is this innovation a sustaining improvement (which incumbents win) or a disruptive innovation (which we win)?

## 12. REAL-WORLD INDIAN PAINT FIELD EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Disrupting the Exterior Primer Market with a 2-in-1 Hybrid
**Situation:** In rural Hadoti, 65% of budget home builders skipped primer entirely because buying a 20L primer pail + 20L topcoat pail was too expensive upfront. They applied cheap distemper directly onto raw cement, causing catastrophic peeling within 4 months. Multinationals ignored this market, viewing budget customers as unprofitable.
**Christensen Disruption Applied:**
- Formulated **Swatch Smart-Shield**: A high-solids, flexible acrylic coating that penetrates raw plaster like a primer while curing into an opaque, weather-resistant matte exterior finish.
- Priced at ₹145/L, offering a 35% total project cost saving compared to the traditional 2-bucket system.
- Contractors loved it because they could finish an exterior in 1 day instead of 3 days.
- Over 12 months, Swatch captured 42% of the rural exterior market across Kota and Bundi, generating ₹3.2 Crore in high-margin revenue from customers who previously bought unbranded lime-wash.

## 13. FAILURE MODES

| Failure Mode | Warning Signs | Christensen Root Cause | Immediate Counter-Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Upmarket Drift Too Early** | Adding expensive perfumes, glitter, or glossy packaging to the disruptive product, raising costs. | The natural temptation to drift upmarket. | Maintain low-end discipline: Protect the lean cost structure; launch separate premium lines for upmarket tiers. |
| **Quality Degradation Trap** | Cutting binder levels so low that the paint chalks off in 60 days. | Confusing low cost with defective quality. | The product must be "Good Enough" to nail the Job-to-be-Done with complete reliability. |
| **Awakening the Incumbent Giant** | Launching aggressive anti-competitor advertising in national media, inviting a price war. | Arrogance and lack of stealth. | Fly beneath the radar: Scale quietly through local contractor networks without provoking defensive incumbent price-slashing. |
